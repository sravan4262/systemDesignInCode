# A. Openers and motivation

The first 5 minutes and the 'why' questions. They test whether you have a clear, forward-looking story about your career.

⭐ = asked in almost every loop. **Story** = the story id that answers it (❓ gap = no story yet). Back to [001-start-here](001-start-here.md); technical "defend this line" questions are in [012-resume-deep-dive](012-resume-deep-dive.md).

## General questions
| # | Question | What they're testing | Story |
|---|---|---|---|

### A7 answer (my words, 2026-11-24, saved as is)
> **Strengths:** communication. I'm open to change, I like change, I like working on new things, and I like taking on more ownership and building bigger projects.
>
> **Weaknesses:** I tend to take too much on myself. I've started not doing that, and if it ends up happening, I've started delegating. Also, sometimes I try to find the optimal solution for a problem that doesn't have one, when sometimes simple solutions just work.
- **Grade (A7):** 5.5/10. Both weaknesses are real and senior-appropriate (not clichés), and the first has an action. The strengths are adjectives without proof.
- **To strengthen (❓):**
  - **Strengths: two, each with one line of evidence.** "Communication" needs proof (e.g. you explained the two-deadline conflict to directors (ST25); you wrote ADRs/docs for the SMTP migration). "Taking ownership / building bigger things" → policy-as-code POC → FedRAMP admin (E8), the triage agent (ST6). "I like change" is weak as a strength; turn it into "I ramp up fast on new things" with proof (Azure Gov AI, SwiftUI for Byte AI).
  - **Pick ONE weakness**, and tell it as a mini-story: name it → where it showed up (a specific time you over-owned something, or over-engineered) → what you do now (e.g. "at planning I ask who else can own a piece"; "I start with the simplest thing that meets the requirement and only optimise with evidence") → the result so far.
  - The "optimal solution" one is good for a senior and links to this repo's HLD practice (avoid over-engineering); the "taking on too much" one is the more common, safer pick.
- **Follow-ups to prep:** Give me an example of when that weakness hurt. How do you know the fix is working? What would your manager say your weakness is?
