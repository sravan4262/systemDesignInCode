# Compensation and negotiation basics

Needed at the recruiter screen (expectations) and again at the offer. Back to [001-start-here](001-start-here.md).

## The parts of an offer
| Part | What it is | Negotiable? |
|---|---|---|
| **Level** | Senior vs. staff; sets the band for everything below | Yes, and it matters most: argue it with your scope stories ([015](015-leveling-and-leadership-signals.md)) |
| **Base salary** | Fixed yearly pay | Yes, within the band |
| **Bonus** | Yearly target % | Sometimes |
| **Equity** | RSUs (public companies) or options (startups), vesting over ~4 years | Often the most room |
| **Sign-on bonus** | One-time cash | Often, especially to cover a bonus or equity you'd lose by leaving |

## Rules
1. **Don't name a number first** if you can avoid it; ask for the band. If you must, give a researched range (levels.fyi, peers) with your target at the bottom of it.
2. **Get everything in writing,** with the total yearly value (base + bonus + equity per year).
3. **Competing offers are your strongest lever.** Time your processes so offers land close together.
4. **Ask, don't demand:** "Is there flexibility on the equity? Company X is at <number>."
5. **Negotiate once, on everything together** (level, base, equity, sign-on), not one item at a time.
6. **Timing:** ask for a few days to decide; don't accept on the call.
